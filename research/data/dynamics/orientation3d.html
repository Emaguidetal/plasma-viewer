Laser perpendicular vs parallel to the RF field, in 3D

Geometry. WR-284: broad dimension a = 72 mm along x, narrow gap b = 34 mm along y, cavity axis along z. In the TE10 mode the RF electric field points only along y. It runs across the short 34 mm gap, from one broad wall to the other, and is strongest at the centre: |E| ∝ sin(πx/a). It forms a standing wave along z. Red arrows point to +y and blue to −y; arrow length is the local |E|. Perpendicular (experiment, Magid et al. IPSTA slides 9 and 13): the laser enters through the narrow side walls and crosses the guide along x at mid-height. The camera looks down through the broad wall. Parallel: the laser enters through a hole in the broad wall and runs along y, along E, through the field maximum.

What this does and does not show. The RF field and plasma are computed together by a 3D Maxwell (Yee FDTD) / collisional-radiative fluid model of the WR-284 cavity at 200 psig He. The plasma current feeds back on the field. The plasma can only grow on mesh nodes that the laser seeded. Diffusion, drift and streamer growth of a rod along E away from the laser line are not modelled in 3D. The experiment shows this growth (front-view frames, slides 13 and 14), and the axisymmetric channel-growth model covers it separately. The 1 mm mesh averages the 20 µm seed; field enhancement at sub-mm channel tips is unresolved. The seed kernel is empirical. This is not a calibrated reproduction of the measured images.
